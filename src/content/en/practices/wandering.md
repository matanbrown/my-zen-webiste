For me, the best way to explain what wandering is is through a scene:
a person walking down the street, someone else asks them "where are
you going?", and their answer is "I have no idea." That's really the
whole thing in a nutshell — walking without a destination, without a
"where to," just the "how."

That said, the best way I know to explain what wandering is and what
it's for is to read Yuval Ido Tal's essay, "A Complete Recommendation
for Training in Wandering" — a piece I've come back to many times over
my years of practice.

Tal writes about what he calls "purposefulness-itis obsessivis" — our
compulsive tendency to harness every action to a goal, a purpose, a
"what for." He describes how most of what we call "life" is really
steps on the way to a destination, not the arrival itself — and how,
too often, we neglect the steps in favor of the goal. The essay's
central insight, for me, comes down to one line:

> "The question isn't 'where to?' Maybe the question is 'how?'"

That's exactly the point that connects wandering, for me, to zazen
shikantaza — in both, the moment you stop asking "where," all that's
left is the "how": how I'm walking, how I'm sitting, how I'm present
with what's here.
