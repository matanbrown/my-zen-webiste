Inner Walk was founded by Phra Olarn, a Thai Buddhist monk, on the island
of Koh Phangan, Thailand. At its center is a simple act — walking — as a
way to explore the inner landscape: thoughts, emotions, and bodily
sensations, without trying to change them, just noticing them.

The practice draws on ancient teachings of Buddhism, Taoism, and Zen, but
reframes them in contemporary language and simple, visual metaphors, so
it's accessible even to people without a religious or philosophical
background. The stated goal is to learn to observe our inner experience
more objectively — and from there, to better understand ourselves and
the roots of our suffering: the confusion, delusions, and projections
through which we usually experience reality.

On Koh Phangan there's a four-day retreat (Monday through Thursday),
open to everyone and free of charge — entirely donation-based, with all
facilitators offering their time voluntarily. The daily structure
includes group walks, Dharma talks, and sharing circles, where
participants talk about what came up for them during the walk.

## How it actually works

Unlike classic walking meditation, which mostly focuses on the sensation
of the feet on the ground, Inner Walk uses walking as a broader
framework for attention — the walking itself is really just the
pretext; what actually matters is paying attention to whatever arises
while walking. Some of the teachers involved in the project (including
Yuval "Fluke" Birman) call this training the "awareness muscle" — a
capacity you can train, just like a muscle in the body, in order to live
with more clarity day to day.

## How I got to know it

After meeting [Yuval Plok Birman](/en/inspirations/yuval-plok-birman/) I started
practicing Inner Walk. I found a practice that was physically simple for
me, and at the same time helped me observe my own mind and the thoughts
running through my head. For me it's like sitting in zazen shikantaza,
just while walking. What speaks to me most is that there are no rules —
no "do it this way, do it that way" — just letting whatever comes up,
come up.
