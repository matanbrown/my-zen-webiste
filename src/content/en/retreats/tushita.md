In May 2015 I arrived at Tushita Monastery in Dharamsala. That's where I
first encountered Buddhism.

It was a ten-day silent retreat, during which you learn the fundamentals
of Buddhism and the philosophy behind it. During the retreat I was
introduced for the first time to the Four Noble Truths — the most basic
part of Buddhist teaching. In essence: the world is full of suffering, and
that suffering can be reduced. As part of the teachings I was promised
enlightenment — a solution to suffering — something I look at quite
differently today.

There were a few core concepts I met for the first time: emptiness,
reincarnation, karma. But the most important thing for me — I encountered,
for the first time, compassion, and the importance of helping every living
being in the world, humans and animals alike. It helped me understand a
lot about myself, and in general.
