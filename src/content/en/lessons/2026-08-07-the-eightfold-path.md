The fourth of the Four Noble Truths says there is a path leading to
the end of suffering. That path is the Eightfold Path — not a
checklist to work through in order, but eight directions that develop
together and feed into one another. Tradition groups them into three
clusters: wisdom, ethical conduct, and concentration.

**Wisdom**

**1. Right View** — understanding the Four Noble Truths not as
theory but as an accurate description of how things work: that there
is suffering, that it has a cause, that it can end, and that there is
a path.

**2. Right Intention** — the inner commitment that shapes practice:
the intention to let go of grasping, ill will, and harm — and to make
room for renunciation and compassion instead.

**Ethical Conduct**

**3. Right Speech** — avoiding lying, gossip, harsh or hurtful speech,
and idle chatter. Not restraint for its own sake, but attention to
what words actually do.

**4. Right Action** — avoiding harm to living beings, avoiding
stealing, and avoiding sexual conduct that harms others. The practical
foundation for a life that doesn't add suffering to the world.

**5. Right Livelihood** — choosing work that isn't based on harm — not
trading in weapons, in people, in meat, in intoxicants, or in drugs.
How you make a living is part of the practice, not separate from it.

**Concentration**

**6. Right Effort** — cultivating wholesome states of mind and letting
unwholesome ones weaken — not by force, but with the steady attention
of tending a garden.

**7. Right Mindfulness** — attention to the body, to sensations, to
states of mind, and to the contents of mind, as they are, without
pushing away and without grasping.

**8. Right Concentration** — developing concentration through
meditation, up to states of deep absorption (jhana) that allow for
clearer insight.

***

These eight aren't steps you pass through and leave behind. They lean
on one another — mindfulness supports right view, right view feeds
right intention, and so on, in a circle that keeps developing
throughout practice. This is the practical shape the third truth —
that suffering can end — takes in an actual life, day after day.
