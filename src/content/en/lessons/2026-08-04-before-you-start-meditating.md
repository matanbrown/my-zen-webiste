If you ask people who've never meditated what they expect to get out
of it, the answers are probably going to sound similar.

To stop thinking.

To relax.

To be less stressed.

To be calmer people.

To be happier.

Some of these things can happen, but if you come to meditation only to
achieve them, there's a good chance you'll find yourself frustrated
instead.

Here are a few of the most common myths around meditation.

## "In meditation you stop thinking"

This is probably the most common myth.

In practice, in most types of meditation the thoughts don't disappear.
Sometimes it even feels like they multiply.

The goal of the practice isn't to stop thinking, but to learn to meet
your thoughts without being automatically swept away by them.

## "Meditation always relaxes you"

Sometimes, yes.

But sometimes it's exactly the opposite.

For some people, the first time they truly encounter anxiety, sadness,
boredom, or pain is precisely when they sit down to meditate.

Not because meditation created these feelings, but because for the
first time they've stopped running from them.

## "Meditation will make me a calmer person"

I still get angry.

Still get disappointed.

Still get scared.

The practice hasn't erased these feelings from my life.

What it has changed is my relationship with them.

Sometimes a small gap opens up between what I feel and how I choose to
respond.

## "There's one correct way to meditate"

There are dozens of traditions and hundreds of different methods.

Some have very clear instructions, and each one trains something
slightly different.

Instead of looking for the "best" method, it might be worth asking
which method suits you well enough that you'll actually want to keep
practicing it over time.

## So what is meditation?

If I had to describe it in one sentence, I'd say:

Meditation is an invitation to meet our experience as it is, before we
try to change it.

It's not a promise of peace.

Not a way to stop thinking.

And not a magic solution.

It's simply an opportunity to stop for a moment, observe, and get to
know ourselves a little more closely.

If you've never meditated before, maybe the best thing you can do
right now is stop reading.

Sit for ten minutes.

Not to succeed.

Not to relax.

Just to find out for yourself how it feels.
